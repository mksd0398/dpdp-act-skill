# DPDP Act 2023 full text, Rules 2025 and compliance checklist

Free reference for India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (Act No. 22 of 2023) and the DPDP Rules, 2025.

## At a glance

- Assent: 11 August 2023.
- Rules: notified 13 November 2025; 23 rules, 7 Schedules.
- Compliance deadline: 14 May 2027 for substantive business obligations; Consent Managers 14 November 2026; Board machinery live since 14 November 2025.
- Regulator: Data Protection Board of India; appeals to TDSAT within 60 days.
- Lawful bases: two, consent (s.6) or certain legitimate uses (s.7). No legitimate interests.
- Child: under 18 (s.2(f)). Targeted ads to children banned (s.9(3)).
- Breach: every breach to the Board and each affected person; Board report within 72 hours (Rule 7). No harm threshold (s.8(6)).
- Maximum penalty: Rs 250 crore (s.8(5) security safeguards).

## Myths and what the Act says

- Myth: We can rely on legitimate interests. The Act: There are only two lawful bases: consent (s.6) or the closed list of certain legitimate uses (s.7). Legitimate interests does not exist under the DPDP Act.
- Myth: Health and financial data are sensitive personal data. The Act: The DPDP Act has no sensitive data category at all. Sectoral law may still add duties.
- Myth: A child means anyone under 13. The Act: A child is anyone under 18 (s.2(f)), and processing needs verifiable parental consent (s.9(1)).
- Myth: We only report breaches that could cause harm. The Act: There is no harm threshold. Every breach goes to the Board and to each affected person (s.8(6)), and a ransomware lockout counts (s.2(u)).
- Myth: Every company needs a Data Protection Officer. The Act: Only notified Significant Data Fiduciaries must appoint one (s.10(2)(a)). Everyone else publishes a contact person (s.8(9)).
- Myth: Indian personal data has to stay in India. The Act: The Act does not mandate localisation. Transfers are allowed unless a country is restricted under s.16(1), though sector rules such as the RBI's still apply (s.16(2)).
- Myth: Affected users can sue us for compensation. The Act: Penalties go to the Consolidated Fund of India (s.34) and civil courts are barred (s.39). The Act gives individuals no compensation right.
- Myth: Parental consent lets us target ads at teenagers. The Act: Tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising directed at children are banned outright, consent or not (s.9(3)).
