Free reference for India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (Act No. 22 of 2023) and the DPDP Rules, 2025.

## At a glance

- Assent: 11 August 2023.
- Rules: notified 13 November 2025; 23 rules, 7 Schedules.
- Compliance deadline: 14 May 2027 for substantive business obligations; Consent Managers 14 November 2026; Board machinery live since 14 November 2025.
- Regulator: Data Protection Board of India; appeals to TDSAT within 60 days.
- Lawful bases: two, consent (s.6) or certain legitimate uses (s.7). No legitimate interests.
- Child: under 18 (s.2(f)). Targeted ads to children banned (s.9(3)).
- Breach: every breach to the Board and each affected person; Board report within 72 hours (Rule 7). No harm threshold (s.8(6)).
- Maximum penalty: Rs 250 crore (s.8(5) security safeguards).

## Ten things most people get wrong

1. There are only two lawful bases: consent, or the closed section 7 list. There is no legitimate interests ground.
2. There is no sensitive personal data category.
3. A child is anyone under 18, not 13 or 16.
4. Targeted advertising to children is banned outright; consent does not cure it.
5. Breach notification has no harm threshold, and covers loss of access.
6. Only notified Significant Data Fiduciaries need a DPO.
7. The Act does not mandate data localisation.
8. Individuals get no compensation; penalties go to the Consolidated Fund.
9. There is no portability right and no right against automated decisions.
10. Data Principals have enforceable duties, with a Rs 10,000 penalty.
