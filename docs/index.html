Commencement

When does the DPDP Act come into force?

In three tranches. 14 May 2027 is the date that matters for a compliance programme.

  1. 14 Nov 2025Board machineryRules 1, 2 and 17 to 21. The Data Protection Board can be constituted.
  2. 14 Nov 2026Consent ManagersRule 4. Registration and obligations of Consent Managers.
  3. 14 May 2027Everything elseRules 3, 5 to 16, 22 and 23: notice, security, breach reporting, retention, children, SDF duties, rights and transfers.

> Do we need a DPO in India?

Using skill: dpdp-analyze

No, unless the Central Government has notified you as a Significant Data Fiduciary.

Only SDFs must appoint a DPO, based in India and responsible to the board s.10(2)(a).

Everyone else publishes a contact person who can answer questions s.8(9) Rule 9.

Trap: size alone does not make you an SDF s.10(1).

Myth vs the Act

What most people, and most AI tools, get wrong

Tap a card to see what the statute actually says.

Myth

“We can rely on legitimate interests.”

What the Act says

There are only two lawful bases: consent (s.6) or the closed list of certain legitimate uses (s.7). Legitimate interests does not exist under the DPDP Act.

Myth

“Health and financial data are sensitive personal data.”

What the Act says

The DPDP Act has no sensitive data category at all. Sectoral law may still add duties.

Myth

“A child means anyone under 13.”

What the Act says

A child is anyone under 18 (s.2(f)), and processing needs verifiable parental consent (s.9(1)).

Myth

“We only report breaches that could cause harm.”

What the Act says

There is no harm threshold. Every breach goes to the Board and to each affected person (s.8(6)), and a ransomware lockout counts (s.2(u)).

Myth

“Every company needs a Data Protection Officer.”

What the Act says

Only notified Significant Data Fiduciaries must appoint one (s.10(2)(a)). Everyone else publishes a contact person (s.8(9)).

Myth

“Indian personal data has to stay in India.”

What the Act says

The Act does not mandate localisation. Transfers are allowed unless a country is restricted under s.16(1), though sector rules such as the RBI's still apply (s.16(2)).

Myth

“Affected users can sue us for compensation.”

What the Act says

Penalties go to the Consolidated Fund of India (s.34) and civil courts are barred (s.39). The Act gives individuals no compensation right.

Myth

“Parental consent lets us target ads at teenagers.”

What the Act says

Tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising directed at children are banned outright, consent or not (s.9(3)).

The DPDP Act at a glance

Full name
The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (Act No. 22 of 2023)
Assent
11 August 2023
Rules
DPDP Rules, 2025, notified 13 November 2025: 23 rules, 7 Schedules
Deadline
14 May 2027 for substantive business obligations
Regulator
Data Protection Board of India; appeals to TDSAT
Lawful bases
Two: consent (s.6) or certain legitimate uses (s.7)
Child
Anyone under 18 (s.2(f))
Breaches
Every breach, to the Board and each affected person; Board report within 72 hours (Rule 7)
Max penalty
Rs 250 crore, for security failures (s.8(5))
